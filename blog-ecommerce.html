eCommerce Store Not Converting? Here's What to Fix First

"We're getting visitors, but nobody's buying" is one of the most common messages store owners send. The instinct is usually to run more ads. But more traffic to a store with a conversion problem just means more people leaving without buying — faster.

Before spending another rupee on ads, work through these in order.

1. Product pages: do they actually answer questions?

A converting product page needs clear photos from multiple angles, a description that answers real questions (size, material, what's included, delivery time), and visible pricing with no surprise costs at checkout. If a visitor has to message you to ask something the page should already answer, that's a conversion leak.

2. Checkout: how many steps does it actually take?

Every extra field, extra page, or forced account-creation step loses buyers. Guest checkout, minimal form fields, and clear delivery/payment options (including COD, which still matters a lot for Pakistani buyers) reduce drop-off significantly.

3. Trust signals: would a stranger buy from you?

No reviews, no clear contact info, no return policy — a new visitor has no reason to trust an unfamiliar store with their money. Even a handful of real reviews, a visible WhatsApp contact, and a simple return/exchange policy noticeably change buyer confidence.

4. Mobile experience: is it actually usable on a phone?

Most traffic is mobile. Slow-loading images, tiny buttons, and checkout forms that are painful to fill on a small screen kill sales that desktop testing would never catch.

5. Inventory and listings: are they even accurate?

Out-of-stock items still showing as available, wrong prices, or outdated photos erode trust fast — and cost you the sale the moment a buyer notices the mismatch.

Only then: traffic and ads

Once the store itself converts well, traffic becomes worth paying for. Fixing the store first is almost always cheaper — and faster — than trying to out-spend a leaky funnel.

Ads can bring people to a broken store. They can't fix it.
