What Does an Executive Assistant Actually Do? A Founder's Guide

"Executive Assistant" gets thrown around loosely, so it helps to be specific about what the role is actually built for — and it isn't just "answering emails."

What an EA actually handles

  • Calendar management — not just booking meetings, but protecting focus time and resolving scheduling conflicts before they become your problem.
  • Inbox triage — separating what needs your personal reply from what can be handled, delegated, or filed.
  • Meeting prep and follow-up — agendas going in, action items and follow-ups coming out, so nothing said in a meeting evaporates afterward.
  • Travel and logistics coordination — bookings, itineraries, and the small details that turn into big problems when missed.
  • Document handling — organizing, formatting, and preparing materials before you need them, not after you ask.

What it isn't

An EA isn't a general task-runner for personal errands (that's closer to a Personal Assistant), and it isn't a project manager running your team's workflows (that's Operations Coordination). The EA role specifically sits close to decision-making — protecting your time and attention so you're only involved in what genuinely needs you.

Signs it's time to bring one in

  1. You're the bottleneck on scheduling — meetings get delayed because you haven't replied.
  2. Important emails sit unanswered because you're buried in low-priority ones.
  3. You walk into meetings without having seen the agenda.
  4. Follow-ups fall through because nobody owns tracking them.

If two or more of these sound familiar, the cost isn't really "admin work" — it's the opportunities and decisions that get delayed because your time is going to the wrong things.

The right EA doesn't just save you hours. They give you back your best hours.
